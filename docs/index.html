01 — the process

Seven steps, in the order that decides whether it works

Skipping forward is how interfaces end up beautiful and wrong, or correct and forgettable. Two of the seven are gates: the process stops, and a person decides. Where that happens, this page turns to paper.

  1. 00

    Mode

    A self-contained artifact, or a screen inside a real codebase. It changes what "done" means for all seven steps.

    Fidelity

    01-fidelity.md

02 — motion, running

Motion has to be earned. Here is the argument, running.

Four things the motion playbook teaches, built as working examples instead of written as claims. Press them. A page that sells motion craft and then only describes it has made the weaker version of its own case.

06-motion-spec.md
9accepted, each with its purpose, curve and duration
10refused, each with the gate that killed it

The refusal list ships as part of the deliverable. It is the half that proves the other half was a decision.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so this page's entrances are switched off. The four examples below still run, because they only move when you press them — that is the difference between motion you asked for and motion you did not.

A

Press feedback

none
140ms ease-out
400ms ease-in

Feedback lands in 140ms. The third one starts slow and finishes late — ease-in delays exactly the moment you are watching, which is why the playbook forbids it on UI.

B

Curve, at the same duration

linear
cubic-bezier

Both take 520ms. The lower one arrives sooner and settles — cubic-bezier(.23, 1, .32, 1). Duration is not what makes motion feel fast; the curve is.

C

Stagger

30–80ms apart reads as one arrival. At 0ms it is a jump with no sequence; at 200ms you are watching a queue and the last tile is late.

D

Interrupting it

CSS transition
CSS keyframes

Flip both back mid-travel. The transition retargets from wherever it is; the keyframe restarts from zero and visibly jumps. That is the whole reason anything fired rapidly uses transitions.

STATUS.md is the page for everyone else

Which step is done, which is waiting, who it is waiting on, and a link to every artifact. One page, written for the person paying for the work rather than the one doing it — so nobody has to ask where things stand.

The two gate files are the ones that bind

A gate file records its own state: pending, human-approved, or policy-auto-selected with the grant that permitted it. An auto-selected decision is provisional by construction — every render stays on disk so a person can come back and overrule it. That is not a formality. It is what happened to this page.

04 — the honest part

The first build of this page was rejected at the gate

Version 1.0 of this site shipped on the specimen-sheet direction. It passed every measurable check: contrast, keyboard, reduced motion, three viewports, no overflow, no dead controls. It scored 7.8. Its lowest dimension was craft, at 7 — and the review passed it anyway, because the total looked respectable.

Then the owner looked at the live page.

It reads as a wireframe — a spec document, not a designed website. Almost no colour. The motion is invisible on first load. One even rhythm all the way down. It has to read as a product somebody invested in. Đức · 2026-08-24 · on the live v1.0 page

He was right, and the gate is what let him say so. The page you are reading is v2, rebuilt on the direction that lost the first gate — with the two AI defaults it originally carried, a gradient headline and a cool neon accent, refused in writing. Three patches went into the skill the same day: the three-second test became a hard floor, craft below 8 now blocks a public deliverable, and a restrained direction now owes one invested moment per viewport.

Shipping a page that was never shown to a person would have been the failure. Being overruled is the process working.

06 — who it is for

Who this is for

The references are the product. The seven steps are how they get used, in order.

People learning the craft

The references are written to be read, not just executed. Decision orders, thresholds, and the reasoning behind them — including the parts that tell you to stop and build nothing.

People shipping real work

A repeatable process you can point an agent at, with an artifact trail a client can follow and two places where you keep control of the outcome.
